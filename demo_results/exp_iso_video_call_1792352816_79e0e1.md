# Experiment Report: ISO Download + Interactive Call
**Scenario ID**: `iso_video_call` | **Date**: 2026-10-18 19:46:56 UTC

### Objective
A large ISO download starts while an interactive video call is active. Evaluates whether QoS protects call latency and jitter from bufferbloat.

### Baseline vs. Adaptive QoS Comparison

| Metric | No QoS (FIFO) | Static QoS | Adaptive QoS (TataElexy) | Improvement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Call Rtt Ms** | 69.30 | 17.00 | 12.70 | ▼ 81.7% reduction |
| **Call Jitter Ms** | 28.40 | 2.10 | 1.40 | ▼ 95.1% reduction |
| **Call Packet Loss Pct** | 4.20 | 0.00 | 0.00 | ▼ 100.0% reduction |
| **Bulk Throughput Mbps** | 46.60 | 22.00 | 42.50 | -8.8% |
| **Queue Depth Packets** | 228 | 25 | 9 | — |
| **Provenance** | 10-trial discrete queueing sample mean | 10-trial discrete queueing sample mean | 10-trial discrete queueing sample mean | — |

### Key Findings
Across 10 workload trials, No QoS suffered severe bufferbloat (69.3 ms RTT). Adaptive QoS reduced call latency by 81.7% (to 12.7 ms) using FQ-CoDel queue isolation while enabling bulk traffic to borrow surplus bandwidth (42.5 Mbps), substantially outperforming Static QoS which rigid-capped bulk at 22.0 Mbps.
